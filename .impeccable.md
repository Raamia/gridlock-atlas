## Design Context

### Users
ShellHacks judges, Sperry Tech sponsors and other first-time viewers, usually watching a three-minute demo or clicking around once. They must understand what they're looking at and what they can do with no training. Transmission planners who dig deeper are a secondary audience: the depth stays, but behind progressive disclosure.

### Brand Personality
Ruled, legible, accountable. Every claim is cited, so the interface should feel like a careful set of engineering drawings, not a sales dashboard. Emotional goal: trust and clarity, never hype.

### Aesthetic Direction
A mix of engineering drawing and control room, on a dark theme.
- Drawing: title-block headings (Barlow Semi Condensed), 1px ruled lines, small radii, solid sheets instead of frosted glass.
- Control room: dark, high-contrast status, calm colour. No neon glow.
- Type: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for all reading text, Barlow Semi Condensed for headings and labels, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono only for identifiers.
- Colour: blueprint-tinted OKLCH neutrals (hue ~250). Utility A is steel blue and utility B is muted mauve, both low chroma. Amber is the only saturated accent and means "overlap".
- Anti-references: cyan/violet glow dashboards, glassmorphism, letter-spaced mono eyebrows, chip clusters, hero-metric blocks, card-in-card, side-stripe borders.

### Design Principles
1. Nothing important hides in the margins. Every action is a visible, labelled control.
2. One idea per surface. Don't cram legend, axis and data into one strip.
3. Plain language first, codes second ("6.7 mi apart · both in service 2028" before "GEO · P92").
4. Ruled lines instead of cards and glow.
5. The user decides how much room each panel gets (resizable rail, inspector and timeline).
